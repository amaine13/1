The Book

Who's Driving Your Bus?

A Practical Guide to Loving Yourself—For Real

A book about recognizing the parts of yourself that take charge, understanding what they are protecting, and finding your way back to the driver's seat.

Read the Introduction

The passengers and the True Self

The Fear Maker, the Pleaser, the Judge, and the other passengers developed as ways to cope and stay safe. Through the bus metaphor, readers learn to trace where those voices came from and meet them with compassion rather than judgment. The True Self is the steadier voice that knows what really matters.

How the book progresses

The book moves from meeting the passengers to exploring their emotional origins, practicing taking the wheel, and living from the driver's seat as an ongoing practice. Its narrative voice draws on clinical case material and personal stories.

Bus Stop Practices

At the close of each chapter, a Bus Stop Practice invites readers to notice what is happening inside, name it, and apply the framework. Practices include sitting with a feeling, writing a letter to a passenger, and pausing for honest recognition before reacting. The experiential approach includes the Gestalt Empty Chair technique: entering into dialogue with the parts of yourself.
