Who's Driving Your Bus?

Introduction

By Judy Mahler Steinfeld, LCSW

Do you want more love, joy, happiness, and peace in your life?

Are you struggling with relationships, frustrated at work, feeling disconnected from yourself or trapped in repeating patterns you cannot seem to change? Maybe you are a seeker. You have tried different approaches, read lots of self-help books, talked endlessly with friends. Maybe you are a therapist, coach, or teacher, and have been on the journey within. Yet the same patterns keep appearing. The same struggles, loneliness, arguments. The same fears. A feeling that life is happening to you instead of being the life you really want.

You have been told, more times than you can count, that you should love yourself more. But nobody has ever shown you how.

What this book is about is changing your perception. It is about seeing your life from another seat on your bus and discovering that the same life can look very different from there. It begins with a very important question.

WHO IS DRIVING YOUR BUS?

Until you can answer that question, you may keep ending up on roads you never intended to take, losing things you want, feeling stressed and struggling to keep control, and wondering why.

The Gestalt Switch

Take a look at this picture.

Some people see a young woman first. Others see an old woman. Keep looking until you can see the other one, and notice the moment it changes. Nothing in the picture has changed. What changed is how you see it. This sudden shift, when we see the same thing in an entirely different way, is called a Gestalt switch. Gestalt means the whole picture, and the principles of Gestalt therapy are the foundation of this book.

Once you have seen the second image, you cannot unsee it. You know there is more than one way of seeing, and the new view becomes part of how you perceive. That is how we grow and evolve.

The same thing can happen with your life. You do not necessarily have to change your circumstances. Sometimes, by becoming aware of the passenger who is interpreting those circumstances, you discover an entirely different reality.

Chloe, in her early 40’s, came to see me some time ago. After a series of unsuccessful relationships, she had begun dating a man she genuinely liked. Then this man’s ex-wife called her, and with every word Chloe listened to, her passengers woke up. The Fear Maker whispered: you cannot trust anyone. The Abandoned One shouted: I can’t survive being hurt again. By the time she came to see me, she was ready to end the relationship.

I asked her what really mattered to her, and what was true for her right now, in this relationship, with this person. She was quiet for a long moment. Then she said: In this moment I care about him. He has been showing up in our relationship. I want to keep going, exploring this.

That was not the Fear Maker or the Abandoned One. That was her True Self, finally getting a word in. By sitting with herself she was able to shift perspective and see the bigger picture, and she knew she had a choice to make. Leave. Or love and support herself on a new journey toward what was really important to her, creating a healthy relationship.

Inside each of us is a bus full of passengers, parts of ourselves that boarded along the way to help us survive, cope, and stay safe. None of them are bad. Each one developed for a reason. But when they grab the wheel, they can drive us using old maps and reacting to dangers that may no longer exist. You will meet them in the chapters ahead.

In a playful, metaphorical way, this book shows us how to change seats. You will learn to recognize your passengers, understand what they are protecting, and meet them with curiosity and compassion instead of letting them drive. The goal is not to get rid of these parts of yourself. It is to love them without handing them the wheel.

The Empty Chair

How do you make that switch? Fritz Perls gave us the empty chair, a way of letting conflicting parts of ourselves speak and be heard. We will return to it throughout this book. The goal is awareness, understanding, and eventually integration.

Each time you see a part of yourself from a new angle and bring it home, you become more whole. This book follows that process, one chapter at a time.

This book makes no promise about an outcome. What it offers is a new and better way to live our lives with love. We can choose the driver, even when we cannot choose the outcome. If you do not do it right, please do not blame yourself. Doing it with love is never a failure.

It is for anyone who wants to learn to love themselves for real, and for the therapists, coaches, teachers, and others who help people change and grow.

When I was growing up in the Bronx, my grandmother Sarah lived in the apartment downstairs from us. She often took the bus to the Bronx House Senior Center, and one thing about her fascinated me from the time I was small: she never ran for the bus. Once I said to her: Grandma, hurry! We are going to miss it! She smiled and said something I have never forgotten. Don’t worry. There’s always another bus.

There is always another bus. Another road. Another chance. Another way of seeing things! Who is driving your bus? The answer to that question can change the road you take and what becomes possible from here.

Let’s find out how to do that!
