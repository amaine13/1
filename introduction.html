Who's Driving Your Bus?

Introduction

By Judy Mahler Steinfeld, LCSW

Do you want more love, joy, happiness, peace in your life?

Are you struggling with relationships, frustrated at work, feeling disconnected from yourself or trapped in repeating patterns you cannot seem to change? Probably you are a seeker, have tried different approaches, read lots of self-help books, and talked endlessly with friends. Maybe you have been in therapy. Yet the same patterns keep appearing. The same struggles, loneliness, arguments. The same fears. A feeling that life is somehow happening to you instead of what you really want.

You’ve been told, more times than you can count, that you should love yourself more. But nobody has ever shown you how. And nobody has helped you see how you are actually standing in your own way.

This book is designed to help you create the life you dream of by starting with a very important question:

WHO IS DRIVING YOUR BUS?

And until you answer that question, you may keep ending up going down roads you never intended to go, losing things you actually want, feeling stressed and struggling to keep control and wondering why.

Chloe, in her early 40s, came to see me some time ago. After a series of unsuccessful relationships, she had begun dating someone she genuinely liked. The relationship was going well. She was starting to feel safe with him, seen by him, and she wanted to build a future together. A family.

Then his ex-wife called her.

The ex-wife had things to say. About him. About what he had done during their marriage. About who he really was, or so she claimed. Chloe listened, and with every word, her passengers woke up. Old fears started moving toward the front of the bus. The Fear Maker. The one that whispers: you cannot trust anyone. The Abandoned One, shouting, I can’t survive being hurt again. The part that wanted to trust but needed to protect herself started to pull back before she could be pulled down.

By the time she came to see me, she was ready to end the relationship.

We sat with it for a while. And then I asked her: what really matters to you? Is it what the ex-wife said, your fears, the need to protect yourself from something that has not happened yet? What do you actually want? What is true for you right now, in this relationship, with this person?

She was quiet for a long moment.

Then she said: In this moment, I care about him. He has been showing up in our relationship. I want to keep going, exploring this.

That was not the ex-wife talking. That was not the Fear Maker or the Abandoned One or the passenger that needed to be safe. That was her. Her True Self, finally getting a word in.

Who was driving her bus? That is the question that changed everything. Not because the fear was wrong to show up. It was doing exactly what it was designed to do. But by sitting with herself she was able to see the bigger picture and knew she had a choice to make. Leave. Or love and support herself on a new journey toward what was really important to her – creating a healthy relationship.

That is what this book is about.

It is not about getting rid of the fear, or silencing the voices, or pretending the pain is not real. It is about learning to ask, in the middle of the reaction, in the middle of the spiral: who is driving my bus right now? And is that who I want at the wheel?

The moment you ask that question, the passengers lose their grip. The daze breaks. And for the first time, you have a real choice: see who has been driving, understand where they come from and why they are afraid, and choose to decide for yourself what really matters.

Is it the gossip that matters here, or the relationship? The fear of being hurt again, or the life you actually want to live?

Loving yourself more does not mean telling yourself you are wonderful until you believe it. It means learning to recognize when a frightened passenger has taken the wheel and choosing, instead, to let your True Self drive. It means knowing what you want clearly enough that fear cannot take it from you without your noticing.

That is what this book teaches you to do.

Inside each of us is a bus full of passengers, parts of ourselves that boarded along the way as strategies to help you survive, to cope, to stay safe. Some boarded in childhood. Others climbed on later, at any point in life when something felt threatening or overwhelming. The Fear Maker. The Pleaser. The Judge. The Abandoned One. The Love Addict. None of them are bad. Every single one developed for a reason and was, at some point, doing its best to protect you and help you move forward. You will meet them all in the next chapters.

But when they grab the wheel, they often drive where we do not really want to go. They often create a reality we do not choose. They make decisions from old maps, reacting to dangers that may no longer exist, steering the bus away from the very things we most want.

There is something steadier. Something that was there before the passengers arrived and will still be there when they finally move to the back of the bus. Your True Self. Your innate wisdom. The one who knows what really matters. The one who needs to be driving.

This book is about finding your way back to that seat.

When I was growing up in the Bronx, my grandmother Sarah lived in the apartment downstairs from us. She often took the bus to the Bronx House Senior Center, and one thing about her fascinated me from the time I was small: she never ran for the bus.

If the bus was pulling away from the curb, she did not panic. She did not break into a trot or call out or wave her arms. She simply kept walking calmly, unhurried, as if she already knew something the rest of us did not.

Once I said to her: Grandma, hurry! We are going to miss it! She smiled and said something I have never forgotten. Don't worry. There's always another bus.

At the time it sounded like a simple observation about public transportation. Over the years I came to understand it was something more. A way of moving through a world that rarely goes according to plan without losing yourself in the panic of the moment.

There is always another bus. Another road. Another chance. The journey does not end when someone steps off the train, or when a door closes, or when the bus pulls away before you reach the stop.

But here is the question Sarah did not answer, the one that took me decades to find my way to.

Who is driving your bus? Your state of consciousness. Your life!

Because the answer to that question determines everything else. It determines where the bus goes and what you find when you get there. It determines whether you spend your life reacting from fear or choosing from love. It determines what you hold onto and what you are finally able to let go.

The answer to who is driving your bus is the answer to what really matters in your life.

Let's find out.
