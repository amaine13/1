Who's Driving Your Bus?

A Practical Resource for Personal Growth and Therapeutic Work

Rooted in Gestalt therapy and informed by more than forty years of clinical experience, Judy Mahler Steinfeld offers an experiential approach to understanding the different parts of ourselves.

Through stories, guided awareness, dialogue, and practical exercises, Who's Driving Your Bus? invites readers and helping professionals to notice familiar patterns, explore different perspectives, and meet themselves with compassion.

An Invitation to Look Again

What do you see?

Take a moment with each picture. What catches your eye first? Can you find another way to see it?

There's no rush. Look again, and notice what happens when your perspective changes.

What Does This Have to Do With the Bus?

The picture hasn't changed. Your perspective has.

Each passenger on our bus tends to see life from its own seat. The Fear Maker may see danger. The Pleaser may see a need to keep everyone happy. But what happens when we move to another seat and look again?

Judy introduces these pictures as a simple way to experience a shift in perception. In Gestalt therapy, awareness and experiential approaches such as chair work can help us explore conflicting perspectives, understand what we feel, and make room for a fuller experience.

We don't have to push our passengers away. We can learn to recognize them, listen to them with compassion, and choose who is driving.

These pictures illustrate changes in visual perception. They are an introduction to perspective-taking, not Gestalt therapy techniques in themselves.

The Passengers

Who is driving your bus right now?

The Judge, the Pleaser, the Fear Maker. Do any of them sound familiar? Judy calls these familiar reactions our passengers.

The Judge

Finds the flaw before anyone else can. Criticizes first to stay one step ahead of rejection.

The Pleaser

Keeps everyone comfortable while quietly abandoning itself. Confuses being needed with being loved.

The Fear Maker

Scans constantly for danger, even when the danger is long gone. Its vigilance once kept you safe.

Meet More Passengers

Underneath All of It

The passengers are not who you are. They are what happened to you.

There is something steadier. Something that was there before the passengers arrived and will still be there when they finally move to the back of the bus.

Your True Self. Your innate wisdom. The one who knows what really matters. The one who needs to be driving.

This book is about finding your way back to that seat.

There is always another bus. Another road. Another chance.
