The Bus Framework

Who's Driving Your Bus?

A Practical Guide to Loving Yourself—For Real

Do you want more love, joy, happiness, peace in your life?

Maybe you’ve read the books, talked endlessly with friends, even been in therapy, and yet the same patterns keep appearing.

This book begins with one very important question:
Who is driving your bus?

The Passengers

Who is driving your bus right now?

Inside each of us is a bus full of passengers, parts of ourselves that boarded along the way as strategies to help us survive, to cope, and to stay safe.

The Judge

Finds the flaw before anyone else can. Criticizes first to stay one step ahead of rejection.

The Pleaser

Keeps everyone comfortable while quietly abandoning itself. Confuses being needed with being loved.

The Fear Maker

Scans constantly for danger, even when the danger is long gone. Its vigilance once kept you safe.

The Love Addict

Clings tightly out of the terror that love will disappear — and in clinging, sometimes pushes it away.

The Hider

Believes invisibility is the safest way to move through life. It stays unknown to stay unhurt.

The Pretender

Covers the flaws, performs the competence. If they knew who I really was, they would leave. Under the mask lies vast loneliness.

None of them are bad. Every single one developed for a reason and was, at some point, doing its best to protect you.

Why This Is Different

Knowing isn't enough. And you already know that.

You’ve been told, more times than you can count, that you should love yourself more. But nobody has ever shown you how.

Loving yourself more does not mean telling yourself you are wonderful until you believe it. It means learning to recognize when a frightened passenger has taken the wheel and choosing, instead, to let your True Self drive.

Underneath All of It

The passengers are not who you are. They are what happened to you.

There is something steadier. Something that was there before the passengers arrived and will still be there when they finally move to the back of the bus.

Your True Self. Your innate wisdom. The one who knows what really matters. The one who needs to be driving.

This book is about finding your way back to that seat.

There is always another bus. Another road. Another chance.

About Judy

The bus framework didn't begin as a book. It grew out of forty years of sitting with people.

Judy Mahler Steinfeld, LCSW, trained at Columbia University and the Gestalt Center for Psychotherapy and Training in New York City, where she later served on faculty. Her approach has always been experiential rather than analytical — less interested in helping people understand their patterns from the outside than in helping them encounter those patterns directly, feel what they feel in the body, and find a way through.
