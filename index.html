The Bus Framework

Meet your passengers.

Inside each of us is a bus full of passengers — parts of ourselves that developed to help us survive, cope, and stay safe. The Fear Maker. The Pleaser. The Judge. None of them are bad. Every single one developed for a reason.

Explore the Framework

From Knowing to Practice

Knowing isn't enough. And you already know that.

You’ve been told, more times than you can count, that you should love yourself more. But nobody has ever shown you how. Loving yourself more does not mean telling yourself you are wonderful until you believe it. It means learning to recognize when a frightened passenger has taken the wheel.

The Book

Who's Driving Your Bus?

A Practical Guide to Loving Yourself—For Real

Readers meet their passengers, understand why they developed, and learn to recognize when one has taken the wheel. The work is not to silence them or push them off the bus, but to return decision-making to the True Self — the part that knows what really matters.

Bus Stop Practices

Each chapter closes with a Bus Stop Practice: an opportunity to pause, reflect, and bring the framework into real life. Some practices invite readers to sit with a feeling, write to a passenger, or notice what happens before reacting.

About Judy

The bus framework didn't begin as a book. It grew out of forty years of sitting with people.

Judy Mahler Steinfeld, LCSW, trained at Columbia University and the Gestalt Center for Psychotherapy and Training in New York City, where she later served on faculty. Her approach has always been experiential rather than analytical — less interested in helping people understand their patterns from the outside than in helping them encounter those patterns directly, feel what they feel in the body, and find a way through.
