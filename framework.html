The Bus Framework

You are meant to be the driver.

Inside each of us rides a bus full of passengers: parts formed as adaptations to fear, pain, and uncertainty. None of them are villains. When they grab the wheel, life starts to feel reactive. This framework changes that.

The passengers are not who you are. They are what happened to you.

Why It Works

You do not need to know what a “part” is. You have ridden a bus.

Clinical language can be a barrier. The bus metaphor is immediately human. Once you can see which passenger is at the wheel, you can begin to make a different choice.

The Passengers

Six you may recognize immediately.

01

The Judge

Finds the flaw before anyone else can. Criticizes first to stay one step ahead of rejection.

02

The Pleaser

Keeps everyone comfortable while quietly abandoning itself. Confuses being needed with being loved.

03

The Fear Maker

Scans constantly for danger, even when the danger is long gone. Its vigilance once kept you safe.

04

The Love Addict

Clings tightly out of the terror that love will disappear — and in clinging, sometimes pushes it away.

05

The Wall Flower

Presses itself to the edges of the room. Invisible feels survivable — and no one can reach what they cannot see.

06

The Pretender

Covers the flaws, performs the competence. If they knew who I really was, they would leave.

Twenty-one passengers are named in the book.

They can ride. They just cannot drive.

A Sample Practice

Who is at the wheel right now?

Think of a recent moment when you reacted in a way you did not intend. Ask: which passenger was driving? Where did you feel it in the body? What was that passenger trying to protect you from?

That pause is where change begins. Early Readers meet the full Bus Stop Practices in the introduction and Chapters 1–3.
